---
title: Glossary
order: 4
summary: Plain meanings of common solar terms.
---

**kWh (kilowatt-hour):** the unit of electricity on your bill. A 1 kW aircon running for one hour uses about 1 kWh.

**kW / kWp (kilowatt-peak):** the size of a solar system, the total rated power of its panels. A 5 kWp system might use nine to ten 550 W panels.

**Price per watt:** total price divided by system size in watts. A PHP 250,000 system of 5 kWp costs PHP 50 per watt. Useful for comparing quotes of different sizes.

**Grid-tie system:** panels and an inverter connected to the utility grid, with no battery. Produces power only in daylight; the grid supplies the rest.

**Hybrid system:** a grid-tie system with a battery. Stores some daytime solar for evening use or brownouts. Costs more.

**Off-grid system:** not connected to the utility. Needs a large battery; mainly for places without reliable grid power.

**Inverter:** the device that converts panel output (DC) into household electricity (AC). Usually needs replacing once during the system's life.

**Net metering:** a utility program that lets you send unused solar power to the grid in exchange for bill credits.

**Self-consumption:** solar power you use in your home as it is produced. Usually the most valuable part of the savings.

**Payback period:** how many years of bill savings it takes to recover the system's price.

**Degradation:** the small yearly drop in panel output, usually about 0.5% a year.

**Workmanship warranty:** the installer's warranty on the installation work itself, separate from equipment warranties.

**Shading:** anything that blocks sunlight on the panels. Even partial shade can cut output noticeably.
